# Waystone World v0.2 — the village, its folklore, and the companion bond

status: ratified in conversation, Crowley + Claude, 2026-08-10 (v0.2 same day: inn-first beats, the wisp, the Great Binder, ease-first navigation)
scope: the Palworld-facing surface ("Waystone", waystone.town) built on the Cairn protocol.
This document is register + lore + onboarding law. Protocol semantics live in the
Protocol_* docs and are unchanged by anything here.

## What Waystone is

The village at the foot of the myth. Waystone is NOT Palworld — Palworld is the
collectors' obsession, their mythological context; Waystone is where that obsession
is kept, shelved, traded, and taken seriously. The tavern, not the war. A fan site,
and proud of the posture.

Emotional target: a user should feel they are entering a place where they can take
their collecting, trading, and playing **seriously — or at least earnestly**.
Sacred the way a first binder is sacred. Never solemn, never theme-park.

## The Law (constitution-grade)

**Muted village, luminous cards.**
Everything of OUR world — buildings, hills, stone, moss, lantern light — stays
earthy, muted, human-scale. The card art is the only thing in any frame allowed
full saturation: little windows into the neighboring world's light.

Corollary A (fidelity contrast, if the pixel direction is chosen): the village may
render at low fidelity (limited-palette pixel) while cards render hi-res and glossy.
The contrast is not a compromise — it dramatizes that the cards come from another
world.

Corollary B: the card-viewing experience is the hero feature of the site. The
village is a frame.

Hard rule: no Pals ever walk the village streets. The myth appears only as
artifacts collectors live with — cards, binders, display niches, posters.
(Register discipline and IP discipline agree here.)

## The world's own strangeness (folklore inventory, v0.1)

- **Stones remember.** In this world, stone is what keeps records. The waystone at
  the village edge carries faint marks that glow ember when something true happens
  (a settlement recorded). The village grew around a stone that remembers — Cairn,
  as physical folklore.
- **Moss is visible memory.** Moss grows where things have been witnessed. Old
  shops are green-shouldered; a newcomer's house is bare stone. Reputation is never
  written as a number in the world art — you can see time-under-witness on surfaces.
  The same visual system governs companion growth: one law of nature everywhere.
- **Reliquary windows.** Architecture built for devotion: houses carry small lit
  glass niches displaying a grail. Houses are part binder; a neighbor's niche is
  their front page. This is WHY the village looks like nowhere else.
- **Night-forest palette, strictly limited.** Deep mosses, slate, charcoal, one
  ember gold — roughly 12–16 colors for the entire world. Cards are the only
  full-spectrum objects.
- **One quiet celestial wrongness.** Something small in the sky says "third place —
  not Earth, not the island." Subtle enough to miss.

## The whisperers (working name)

Small creatures of OUR world who are devoted scholars of the neighboring one.
**They are the connection point between Waystone and Palworld — lean into it.**
They know the creatures there, the cards, the lore, the printings; their expertise
is real (the catalog and record ARE their scholarship). The bond with a collector
is shared obsession: the collector arrives obsessed; the whisperer already was.

- Glimpsed before met: eyes in a knothole, grass rippling against the wind. Never
  shown plainly in the village scene until yours reveals itself.
- **Own identity — it doesn't want to be a pet.** It is a peer with prior devotion,
  not property. All companion UX flows from this dignity.
- Affinity, not likeness: a companion is tinted by the element of what its
  collector loves (ember for Kindling hearts, etc.) — never shaped like a Pal.
- Agents grow CAPABILITY, never AUTHORITY (inherited law): whispers are learned
  advice, judged not enforced, blue not green. Growth feeds only on recorded
  activity — scans, curation, settled trades — never engagement bait.
- Visual design: UNDECIDED. Direction: small, whispers in your ear,
  shoulder-companion register; stone/moss materials belong to this world.

## The bond (sacred onboarding — this IS agent setup)

Never a settings form. The ritual:

1. **Guests browse unwatched.** No listening, no tracking theater. Free forever.
2. **Intent opens the ear.** When a visitor declares they may stay ("linger"), the
   village begins noticing — grass ripples near what they return to. The attention
   is opt-in by intent; by the time the companion speaks, the collector has been
   choosing it for a while. Transparency is absolute: "the waystone remembers" is
   the stated premise, and what the village notices is disclosed plainly.
3. **It speaks first** at the waystone, at night: it heard what they lingered on.
4. **Three questions, conversational** (the answers are the bond AND the persona
   seed carried into every agent call):
   - which one is yours? (favorite Pal, searchable)
   - why? (free text — nobody asks collectors why; we do)
   - how do you keep them? (player / collector / alternate-hunter)
5. **It steps out of the grass** — affinity-tinted by the answers.
6. **The naming is negotiated: it offers names and takes suggestions** until one
   feels right to both. It has its own identity; naming is agreement, not labeling.
7. **The first whisper is a true thing from the record** about the collector's own
   card ("Foxparks has three printings; the SR is the one they hunt") — first
   contact demonstrates expertise, intimacy, and honesty in one line.
8. A modest house stands on the lot by morning. It moves in too.

## Boundaries (inherited + specific)

- Fan-made community; not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by
  Pocketpair, Inc. Card imagery courtesy of Palify, with the operator's permission;
  catalogue images are references, never proof of a seller's copy.
- Full Cairn no-overclaim discipline: records vs claims, green only for recorded,
  the protocol never verifies authenticity or condition.
- No AI-art optics risk with the community: world art is hand-built (code) now;
  any future commission goes to a community artist.


## v0.2 — the arrival, restructured (inn-first)

**Law: paper travels, cardboard doesn't.** Precious cards are never pinned
outdoors. Card art appears ONLY where cards truly live: binders, sleeves,
display cases, reliquary niches, hands at a table. A notice board, if present,
holds paper — sketch-wants, market-day announcements — muted, never luminous.
(The gate notice board is demoted to optional set dressing.)

**The beats:**
1. THE TRAIL — short, one screen. The waystone glows a mark as you pass (the
   village noticed). Among the fireflies on the path, one light is different:
   **the wisp** — your agent in unformed, ethereal form. It found you at the
   stone and leads you to the inn. Design of the dawning: at first it reads as
   a firefly; the tell is BEHAVIORAL — fireflies drift, this one keeps pace,
   waits when you stop, returns when you stray. Only after you notice does the
   visual confirmation grow: steadier light, faint trail. The bond ceremony is
   later revealed as a TAKING-OF-FORM: the three questions are what the wisp
   needs to become; the first whisperer anyone sees fully is their own,
   resolving out of light in front of them.
2. THE DOOR — the inn is open to travelers. No account, no gate.
3. THE COMMON ROOM — the whole first experience: fire, tables, life
   half-glimpsed (companions as shoulder-silhouettes in the gloom, never
   clear), and **the GREAT BINDER** on the reading table: the inn's reference
   album of every printing known to the village. Leafing it is free and
   unwatched — the guest catalogue, wearing the diegetically correct body
   (cards luminous inside pocket pages; the existing pocket-page UI IS the
   album). Rest means no demands.
4. WANTS ARE PEOPLE, NOT PAPER — the village's longing lives in the room:
   Merra is at the corner table and she's hunting Foxparks. The market is
   social by inhabitation, never by feature.
5. THE QUESTION comes when it means something: when a guest tries to MARK a
   card as theirs — "a binder of your own needs a shelf, and a shelf needs a
   house." Conversion is the collector's own gesture.
6. ARRIVAL WITH CARDS — new residents come CARRYING collections. First night
   in the house: lay the cards out, scan them in (the existing pile-scan
   pipeline: one photo per binder page, witnessed frames, copies counted).
   The binder populates from the real shoebox. **The empty pockets do the
   recruiting**: gaps in the pages become wants naturally — marking a want is
   answering an empty pocket. The wisp watches you unpack; the bond ceremony
   follows.

**Navigation: story once, speed forever (EASE OF USE FIRST — Crowley).**
Four core surfaces, four places, one mapping:
- the Great Binder (master catalogue) — at the inn
- your binder (own collection) — at your house
- the shops (per-seller storefronts) — the row of villagers' tables
- the market square (master storefront: everything for sale, one search)
First visit walks the beats; forever after, a persistent compact dock in PLAIN
WORDS: Catalogue · My Binder · Shops · Market (village names are flavor on
hover, never the label). Mobile: bottom tab bar. The bonded companion is the
fifth element of navigation — the one you talk to, present in the dock
everywhere. The village remains the home screen and the place ceremonies
happen; daily work is never more than two taps deep.
